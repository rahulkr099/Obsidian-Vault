
The previous lesson taught us how to **retry failed jobs**.

Now we need to answer a different question:

> **How do we know whether our job system is healthy?**

In development, you can look at:

```text
console.log()
```

In production, that's not enough.

You need to know:

```text
How many jobs are waiting?
How many are running?
How many are failing?
How many are retrying?
Which jobs are slow?
Which workers are unhealthy?
Are queues growing?
```

# 1. Why Job Monitoring Matters

Imagine your AI LMS is running in production.

An instructor clicks:

```text
Generate Quiz
```

The API responds:

```text
Generation started
```

But behind the scenes:

```text
AI Queue
──────────────
Waiting: 8,500
Active: 10
Failed: 2,300
```

Users are now seeing:

```text
"Generating..."
```

for 20 minutes.

Without monitoring, you might not know why.

With monitoring, you immediately see:

```text
🚨 AI queue backlog increasing
🚨 Worker failure rate high
🚨 Provider returning many 429s
```

That's the purpose of monitoring.

# 2. What Should We Monitor?

For your LMS, monitor at least:

```text
Queue health
Worker health
Job counts
Job duration
Failure rate
Retry count
Queue latency
Processing latency
```

Let's understand each one.

---

# 3. Queue States

A BullMQ queue can have jobs in different states.

Conceptually:

```text
             Queue
               │
       ┌───────┼────────┐
       ↓       ↓        ↓
    Waiting  Active   Failed
               │
               ↓
           Completed
```

Important states include:

```text
waiting
active
completed
failed
delayed
```

You want visibility into these states.

---

# 4. Waiting Jobs

`waiting` means:

> Jobs are waiting to be processed.

For example:

```text
AI Queue
Waiting: 150
```

This isn't automatically bad.

Maybe your worker is processing normally.

But:

```text
Waiting: 150
Waiting: 500
Waiting: 2,000
Waiting: 10,000
```

and continuously increasing?

That's a warning sign.

---

# 5. Queue Backlog

Suppose your system receives:

```text
100 jobs/minute
```

but workers process:

```text
50 jobs/minute
```

Then:

```text
Incoming
100/min
   ↓
Queue
+50/min
   ↓
Backlog grows
```

Eventually:

```text
1,000
5,000
10,000
50,000
```

This is called **queue backlog**.

---

# 6. Queue Depth

One useful metric is:

```text
queue depth = number of pending jobs
```

For example:

```text
AI Queue

Waiting: 230
Delayed: 50
Active: 10
```

You could expose an internal monitoring endpoint:

```http
GET /internal/queues
```

Response:

```json
{
  "ai": {
    "waiting": 230,
    "active": 10,
    "delayed": 50,
    "failed": 4
  }
}
```

This is extremely useful during development.

---

# 7. Active Jobs

`active` tells you:

> How many jobs are currently being processed?

Example:

```text
AI Worker
Active: 5
```

If your worker concurrency is:

```ts
concurrency: 10
```

and you consistently see:

```text
Active: 10
Waiting: 5,000
```

your workers are operating at full capacity.

That may indicate the need for:

```text
more workers
or
better processing efficiency
or
provider capacity
```

---

# 8. Failed Jobs

This is one of the most important metrics.

For example:

```text
Email Queue
Failed: 72
```

A few failures may be normal.

But:

```text
Failed:
10
50
500
5,000
```

is a serious signal.

You need to know:

```text
Why are they failing?
Which job type?
Which provider?
Which error?
```

---

# 9. Retry Count

From Lesson 11.10:

```text
Job
 ↓
Attempt 1 ❌
 ↓
Attempt 2 ❌
 ↓
Attempt 3 ✅
```

If many jobs require retries:

```text
Retry rate ↑
```

something might be wrong.

For example:

```text
AI provider
→ 429 errors
```

or:

```text
Database
→ connection failures
```

Retry count is therefore a useful early-warning metric.

---

# 10. Completed Jobs

Don't only monitor failures.

You also want:

```text
Completed jobs
```

For example:

```text
AI Queue
Completed: 4,230
Failed: 20
```

This gives you a basic success/failure picture.

You could calculate:

```text
success rate =
completed / (completed + failed)
```

For example:

```text
4,230 / 4,250
≈ 99.5%
```

---

# 11. Failure Rate

A more useful metric is:

```text
failure rate =
failed jobs / total processed jobs
```

For example:

```text
Processed = 10,000
Failed = 100

Failure rate = 1%
```

Then compare over time.

```text
10:00 → 0.5%
11:00 → 0.6%
12:00 → 1.2%
13:00 → 8.5%
```

That sudden increase should trigger investigation.

---

# 12. Job Duration

Suppose AI generation normally takes:

```text
5 seconds
```

Then suddenly:

```text
30 seconds
```

then:

```text
60 seconds
```

The queue might still be "working," but performance is getting worse.

So measure:

```text
job duration
```

For example:

```text
Average: 6.2 sec
P50: 5.1 sec
P95: 12.4 sec
P99: 30.8 sec
```

---

# 13. What Is P95?

You don't always want only the average.

Imagine 100 jobs:

```text
95 jobs → 2 seconds
5 jobs  → 60 seconds
```

The average might hide the problem.

**P95** means approximately:

> 95% of jobs complete within this duration.

So:

```text
P95 = 12 seconds
```

means most jobs finish within 12 seconds, while the slowest 5% take longer.

For production systems, percentile metrics are often more useful than averages.

---

# 14. Queue Wait Time

There's another important metric:

> How long did the job wait before a worker started processing it?

Example:

```text
Job created
   ↓
wait 30 seconds
   ↓
worker starts
   ↓
processing 5 seconds
```

We have:

```text
Queue wait time = 30 seconds
Processing time = 5 seconds
Total time = 35 seconds
```

These are different problems.

---

# 15. Why This Difference Matters

Suppose:

```text
Processing time = 5 sec
Total time = 60 sec
```

You might think the worker is slow.

But actually:

```text
Waiting = 55 sec
Processing = 5 sec
```

Your real problem is:

```text
Queue capacity
```

not:

```text
AI processing
```

This distinction is very important.

---

# 16. End-to-End Job Latency

For your LMS:

```text
User requests AI quiz
        ↓
Job created
        ↓
Wait in queue
        ↓
Worker starts
        ↓
AI processing
        ↓
Database save
        ↓
Completed
```

You can measure:

```text
Total latency
```

from:

```text
job created
```

to:

```text
job completed
```

This gives you a realistic picture of the user's waiting experience.

# 17. Worker Health

Monitoring isn't only about jobs.

You also need to know:

> **Are workers actually alive?**

Imagine:

```text
API Server ✅
Redis      ✅
PostgreSQL ✅
Worker     ❌
```

The API can still respond:

```text
Generation started
```

but nothing will process the job.

Then:

```text
Waiting:
10
100
1,000
10,000
```

This is why worker health is critical.

# 18. Worker Heartbeat

A worker can periodically update a heartbeat.

Conceptually:

```text
worker-1
lastHeartbeat:
14:30:15
```

Then:

```text
worker-1
lastHeartbeat:
14:30:15
```

If the current time is:

```text
14:35:00
```

you know the worker may be unhealthy.

You don't necessarily need to build a custom heartbeat system immediately; your deployment/process manager and queue infrastructure can provide useful signals. But the concept is important.

# 21. Queue Lag

A useful concept is **queue lag**.

It roughly answers:

> How long are jobs waiting before processing?

For example:

```text
Queue lag = 2 seconds
```

is probably fine.

But:

```text
Queue lag = 5 minutes
```

could be a serious issue for:

```text
AI generation
email verification
notifications
```

# 22. Different Queues Need Different Expectations

Don't use one global threshold.

For example:

```text
Notification:
few seconds may be okay

Email:
maybe seconds/minutes depending on importance

AI generation:
several seconds may be expected

Security email:
should probably be processed quickly
```

Monitoring thresholds should match business importance.

# 32. Job ID vs Request ID

They're not the same thing.

### Request ID

Identifies an HTTP request:

```text
req_123
```

### Job ID

Identifies the background job:

```text
job_456
```

You can relate them:

```text
req_123
   ↓
job_456
```

Then:

```text
req_123
→ user requested quiz generation

job_456
→ worker processed that generation
```

This is excellent for debugging.

# 34. Metrics vs Logs

Don't confuse them.

### Logs

Tell you:

> What happened?

Example:

```text
AI job 123 failed with timeout.
```

### Metrics

Tell you:

> How often/how much?

Example:

```text
AI failure rate = 3.2%
```

### Traces

Tell you:

> Where did the request spend its time?

Example:

```text
API → Queue → Worker → LLM → PostgreSQL
```

For this lesson, focus primarily on logs + metrics.

Tracing becomes a larger observability topic later.

# 47. Graceful Shutdown

Monitoring also helps when deploying new workers.

Suppose:

```text
Worker v1
```

is processing jobs.

You deploy:

```text
Worker v2
```

You don't want to kill v1 in the middle of a job unnecessarily.

A worker should support graceful shutdown:

```text
SIGTERM
 ↓
Stop accepting new work
 ↓
Finish current job
 ↓
Close connections
 ↓
Exit
```

This is an important production behavior.

---

# 48. What Happens Without Graceful Shutdown?

Imagine:

```text
AI job
 ↓
Worker killed
```

The job might:

```text
fail
retry
```

or produce an ambiguous state depending on when the process died.

Graceful shutdown reduces unnecessary failures.

This becomes especially important when deploying with Docker, Kubernetes, or process managers.

# 🧠 Interview Questions

### What is queue monitoring?

Monitoring the state and performance of background jobs and workers.

### What is queue depth?

The number of jobs waiting to be processed.

### Why is queue depth important?

A continuously growing queue can indicate that incoming work is greater than worker processing capacity.

### What is job latency?

The time taken for a job to move through the processing lifecycle.

### What is the difference between queue wait time and processing time?

Queue wait time is how long the job waits before processing; processing time is how long the worker takes to execute it.

### Why monitor failed jobs?

A growing failure count can indicate bugs, provider outages, configuration problems, or infrastructure issues.

### Why monitor retries?

A high retry rate can indicate temporary failures becoming widespread, such as rate limiting or provider instability.

### Why do workers need health monitoring?

A dead worker can leave jobs stuck in the queue even though the API itself is still functioning.

### Why are structured logs useful?

They make it easier to search and correlate job activity using fields such as job ID, queue, attempt, and error type.

### Why shouldn't queue monitoring be public?

Queue statistics and job information are internal operational data and may expose sensitive implementation details.
