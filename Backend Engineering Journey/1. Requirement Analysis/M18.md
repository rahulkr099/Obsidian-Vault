> **Goal:** Learn how to identify, evaluate, and reduce risks before development begins. Professional backend engineers don't assume everything will go perfectly—they actively plan for what could go wrong and prepare mitigation strategies.

# 1. What is Risk Analysis?

**Risk Analysis** is the process of identifying events that could negatively affect a project or system and deciding how to reduce their impact.

A risk answers:

> **"What could go wrong, how likely is it, and what will we do about it?"**

# 13. Risk Assessment Matrix

Each risk can be evaluated using:

- **Likelihood** (How likely?)
    
- **Impact** (How serious?)
    

|Likelihood|Impact|Priority|
|---|---|---|
|High|High|Critical|
|High|Low|Medium|
|Low|High|High|
|Low|Low|Low|

Focus on high-impact risks first.

---

# 14. Risk Register

Professional teams maintain a **Risk Register**.

Example:

|Risk|Category|Likelihood|Impact|Mitigation|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Database overload|Performance|High|High|Add indexes and caching|
|Email service outage|External|Medium|Medium|Retry with queue|
|Duplicate payments|Business|Low|High|Idempotency keys|
|JWT theft|Security|Medium|High|Short-lived tokens and refresh flow|

This document is updated throughout the project.

# 17. Mitigation vs Contingency

These are different concepts.

### Mitigation

Actions taken **before** the risk happens to reduce the chance or impact.

Example:

```text
Add Database Indexes
```

to reduce slow queries.

---

### Contingency

Actions taken **after** the risk occurs.

Example:

```text
Restore Database

From Backup
```

after a failure.

Professional teams prepare both.

# 20. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1

Ignoring low-probability, high-impact risks.

Example:

Database corruption.

Rare, but serious.

---

## Mistake 2

Only identifying risks without mitigation plans.

A risk list alone is not enough.

---

## Mistake 3

Assuming third-party services never fail.

They do.

Plan for it.

---

## Mistake 4

Ignoring operational risks.

Deployment and infrastructure matter as much as application code.

---

## Mistake 5

Never revisiting the risk register.

Risks change as the project evolves.

---

# 21. Risk Analysis Checklist

For every project, ask:

- What could go wrong?
    
- How likely is it?
    
- How serious would it be?
    
- How can we reduce the likelihood?
    
- How can we reduce the impact?
    
- How will we detect it?
    
- What is the recovery plan?

# Interview Questions

### Q1. What is risk analysis?

**Answer:**

Risk analysis is the process of identifying potential problems that could affect a project, evaluating their likelihood and impact, and planning strategies to reduce or manage them.

---

### Q2. What is the difference between mitigation and contingency?

**Answer:**

Mitigation consists of preventive actions taken before a risk occurs to reduce its likelihood or impact. A contingency plan defines how the team will respond if the risk actually happens.

---

### Q3. Why should backend engineers care about business risks?

**Answer:**

Backend engineers build systems to support business goals. Understanding business risks helps prioritize the right features, avoid unnecessary complexity, and deliver value more effectively.

---

### Q4. Give examples of technical risks in backend development.

**Answer:**

Examples include inefficient database queries, concurrency bugs, memory leaks, poor API design, data corruption, and dependency compatibility issues.

# Key Takeaways

- Risk analysis is about **preparing for uncertainty**, not predicting the future.
    
- Consider technical, business, security, operational, performance, external service, project, and compliance risks.
    
- Prioritize risks using **likelihood** and **impact**.
    
- Create both **mitigation** and **contingency** plans.
    
- Continuously review risks as the project evolves.
    

---

# Real-World Backend Perspective

Before launching a new feature, experienced backend engineers often ask questions like:

- What happens if the database is unavailable?
    
- What if traffic is 10× higher than expected?
    
- What if the payment gateway times out?
    
- What if a deployment fails?
    
- What if users submit the same request twice?
    
- How do we recover from corrupted data?
    

These discussions happen during design reviews because fixing risks before release is usually much cheaper than fixing production incidents.
