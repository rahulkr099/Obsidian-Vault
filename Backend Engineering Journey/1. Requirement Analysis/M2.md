# Backend Engineering — Requirements Analysis Notes

# Module 2 — Understanding the Business

> **Goal:** Learn how to understand the business before thinking about databases, APIs, or code. A backend engineer builds software to solve business problems, not just technical problems.

---

# Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

- Understand what a business goal is.
    
- Identify the problem an application is trying to solve.
    
- Ask the right business questions.
    
- Convert a business idea into clear technical requirements.
    
- Avoid building unnecessary features.
    

---

# 1. What Does "Understanding the Business" Mean?

Every software application exists for a reason.

Before designing anything, ask:

> **"Why does this application exist?"**

A backend engineer should understand the business just as well as the code.

### Example

A client says:

> "I need a blogging platform."

Don't immediately think about:

- MongoDB
    
- Express
    
- JWT
    
- Docker
    

Instead, think:

```text
Why does the client need it?

↓

Who will use it?

↓

What problem will it solve?

↓

How will success be measured?
```

Technology comes after understanding the business.

# 3. The Four Fundamental Business Questions

Before writing any code, answer these four questions.

## Question 1

### Who is building this?

Examples:

- Startup
    
- School
    
- Hospital
    
- Bank
    
- E-commerce company
    

This tells you the business context.

---

## Question 2

### Who will use it?

Examples:

```text
Guest

Reader

Author

Admin
```

Different users have different needs.

---

## Question 3

### Why does it exist?

Example:

Instead of saying:

```text
To write blogs.
```

Say:

```text
To help writers publish knowledge that readers can easily discover and learn from.
```

This explains the business value.

---

## Question 4

### What problem does it solve?

Never skip this question.

Example:

```text
Problem

↓

Writers struggle to publish articles.

↓

Readers struggle to find quality content.

↓

Solution

↓

Blog Platform
```

# 4. Business Goal vs Technical Goal

Many beginners confuse these.

## Business Goal

Describes **why** the application exists.

Example:

```text
Allow writers to publish articles and help readers discover useful content.
```

---

## Technical Goal

Describes **how** the application will be built.

Example:

```text
Node.js

Express

MongoDB

Redis

Docker
```

Notice:

The business doesn't care whether you use MongoDB or PostgreSQL.

The business cares that users can publish blogs successfully.

# 5. Case Study — Blog Platform

Imagine a client says:

> "Build a blogging platform."

Let's analyze it.

---

## Business Goal

```text
Provide a platform where authors can create, publish, and manage articles while allowing readers to discover, read, and discuss them.
```

---

## Business Problems

```text
Authors need a place to publish content.

↓

Readers need quality content.

↓

Readers need search.

↓

Readers want discussions.

↓

Admins need moderation tools.
```

---

## Business Success

How do we know the application is successful?

Examples:

```text
More published blogs

↓

More active readers

↓

Higher engagement

↓

Lower spam

↓

Faster search
```

Notice these are business outcomes, not technical metrics.

# 6. Features Should Support the Business Goal

Never add features just because they're popular.

Ask:

> Does this feature help achieve the business goal?

### Example

Business Goal:

```text
Help authors publish articles.
```

Useful features:

- Drafts
    
- Rich text editor
    
- Categories
    
- Tags
    
- Search
    
- Comments
    

---

Less useful features:

- Video streaming
    
- Live chat
    
- Online gaming
    

These don't support the business goal.

---

# 7. Ask "Why?" Repeatedly

Good analysts ask "Why?" several times.

### Example

Client:

```text
I need notifications.
```

Ask:

```text
Why?
```

Client:

```text
So users know when someone comments.
```

Ask:

```text
Why?
```

Client:

```text
Because discussions keep readers engaged.
```

Now you understand the real purpose.

# 9. Business Metrics (KPIs)

Businesses measure success using Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).

Examples for a Blog Platform:

|KPI|Why It Matters|
|---|---|
|Registered users|Measures growth|
|Daily active users|Measures engagement|
|Published articles|Measures content creation|
|Average reading time|Measures content quality|
|Comments per article|Measures interaction|
|Search success rate|Measures discoverability|

As a backend engineer, knowing these helps you prioritize performance and features.

# 12. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1

Thinking technology first.

Wrong:

```text
Let's use MongoDB.
```

Correct:

```text
What problem are we solving?
```

---

### Mistake 2

Ignoring business priorities.

Example:

Spending weeks building animations while search is still broken.

---

### Mistake 3

Building features nobody requested.

Every feature increases:

- Development time
    
- Testing effort
    
- Maintenance cost
    

---

### Mistake 4

Not understanding how the company earns money.

Knowing the business model helps you make better engineering decisions.

---

# 13. Checklist Before Moving Forward

Before starting architecture, you should know:

- What problem are we solving?
    
- Who are the users?
    
- Why does the application exist?
    
- What defines success?
    
- What are the business constraints?
    
- Which features directly support the business goal?
    
- What should not be built?
    

If you can't answer these questions, you're not ready to design the system.

# Interview Questions

### Q1. Why should a backend engineer understand the business?

**Answer:**

Because backend systems exist to solve business problems. Understanding the business helps engineers design features, APIs, and databases that meet real user needs instead of making assumptions.

---

### Q2. What is the difference between a business goal and a technical goal?

**Answer:**

A business goal explains **why** the application exists and what value it provides to users or the organization. A technical goal explains **how** the application will be implemented using technologies, architecture, and infrastructure.

---

### Q3. Why is asking "Why?" important during requirements analysis?

**Answer:**

Asking "Why?" helps uncover the real business need behind a requested feature. This prevents unnecessary work and ensures the solution addresses the actual problem.

---

### Q4. Give examples of business constraints.

**Answer:**

Common business constraints include budget, timeline, team size, legal or regulatory requirements, and available resources. These constraints influence technical decisions and project scope.

# Key Takeaways

- Every software project begins with a business problem, not with code.
    
- Understand **who**, **why**, and **what problem** before designing the system.
    
- Business goals should drive every technical decision.
    
- Features that don't support the business goal should usually be postponed or removed.
    
- Strong backend engineers think about the business as much as they think about APIs and databases.
