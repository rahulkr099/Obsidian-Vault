# Module 14 — Lesson 14.3: Keeping Services Loosely Coupled ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn how to design services that can change independently.

**Loose coupling** means:

> A service should know **as little as possible** about another service.

This makes your application easier to maintain and extend.

---

# Tightly Coupled (❌)

```text
PostService

↓

Knows EmailService internals

↓

Knows SMTP settings

↓

Knows email templates
```

If `EmailService` changes, `PostService` also needs changes.

---

# Loosely Coupled (✅)

```text
PostService

↓

EmailService.send(dto)
```

`PostService` only knows:

> "I need to send an email."

It doesn't care **how** the email is sent.

---

# Real Example

Requirement:

> Publish a post.

Good workflow:

```text
PostService

↓

NotificationService.notify(dto)
```

`PostService` doesn't know whether `NotificationService`:

- Sends an email
    
- Sends a push notification
    
- Saves a notification
    
- Uses Firebase
    

Those implementation details are hidden.

---

# Communicate Through Contracts

Think of a service as exposing a **contract**.

Example:

```text
EmailService

↓

send(dto)
```

The caller only needs to know:

- What method to call
    
- What data to pass
    

Not how it's implemented.

---

# Benefits

✅ Easier to replace implementations.

Example:

Today:

```text
EmailService

↓

SMTP
```

Tomorrow:

```text
EmailService

↓

AWS SES
```

`PostService` doesn't change.

---

# Common Mistakes

## ❌ Knowing Internal Details

```text
PostService

↓

SMTP Config

↓

Email Templates

↓

Send Email
```

Too much knowledge.

---

## ✅ Delegate Responsibility

```text
PostService

↓

EmailService
```

That's enough.

---

# Best Practices

✅ Talk through public methods.

✅ Hide implementation details.

✅ Depend on **what a service does**, not **how it does it**.

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** What does "loosely coupled services" mean?

**Good Answer:**

> Loosely coupled services depend only on each other's public behavior, not internal implementation. This allows one service to change without affecting others, making the system easier to maintain and extend.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Keep services independent.
    
- Hide implementation details.
    
- Communicate through clear service methods.
    
- Depend on behavior, not implementation.
