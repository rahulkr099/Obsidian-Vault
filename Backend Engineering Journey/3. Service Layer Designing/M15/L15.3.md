# Module 15 — Lesson 15.3: Shared Business Helpers ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn when to use shared business helpers instead of creating a full service.

Not every reusable piece of business logic needs its own service.

Sometimes, a **shared helper** is enough.

---

# What is a Shared Business Helper?

A shared helper contains **small, reusable business logic**.

Example:

```text
calculateDiscount()

↓

isBusinessDay()

↓

calculateLateFee()
```

These functions can be reused by multiple services.

---

# Example

Instead of:

```text
OrderService

↓

Calculate Discount

----------------

CartService

↓

Calculate Discount
```

Create:

```text
PricingHelper

↓

calculateDiscount()
```

Now both services reuse the same logic.

---

# Helper vs Service

### Use a Helper

When the logic is:

- Small
    
- Stateless
    
- Doesn't need repositories or other services
    

Example:

```text
calculateAge()

formatCurrency()

calculateTax()
```

---

### Use a Service

When the logic:

- Has business workflows
    
- Calls repositories
    
- Calls other services
    

Example:

```text
PaymentService

NotificationService

PermissionService
```

---

# Simple Rule

Ask yourself:

> **Does this logic need to coordinate other components?**

If **No** →

```text
Helper
```

If **Yes** →

```text
Service
```

---

# Best Practices

✅ Keep helpers small.

✅ Don't let helpers access the database.

✅ Use services for business workflows.

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** When would you use a helper instead of a service?

**Good Answer:**

> I use a helper for small, stateless, reusable logic that doesn't depend on repositories or other services. If the logic coordinates business workflows or external components, it belongs in a service.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Helpers are for **small reusable logic**.
    
- Services are for **business workflows**.
    
- Helpers should not access the database.
    
- Choose the simplest abstraction that fits the problem.
