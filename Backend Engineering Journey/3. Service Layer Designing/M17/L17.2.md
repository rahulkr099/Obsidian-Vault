# Module 17 — Lesson 17.2: Composing Multiple Services ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn how one service coordinates multiple services to complete a business workflow.

Think of the **main service** as an **orchestrator**.

It doesn't do every task itself—it delegates work to specialized services.

---

# Example 1 — Blog App

Requirement:

> User publishes a blog post.

Workflow:

```text
PostService

↓

SlugService

↓

PostRepository

↓

SearchService

↓

NotificationService
```

Responsibilities:

- `SlugService` → Generate slug
    
- `PostRepository` → Save post
    
- `SearchService` → Index the post
    
- `NotificationService` → Notify followers

# Main Rule

The **main service** owns the workflow.

Other services own their specialized responsibilities.

```text
Main Service

↓

Service A

↓

Service B

↓

Service C
```

---

# Benefits

Instead of:

```text
One Service

↓

1000 Lines
```

You get:

```text
Small Services

↓

Simple Responsibilities
```

Much easier to maintain.

---

# Best Practices

✅ Keep each service focused.

✅ Let one service coordinate the workflow.

✅ Don't duplicate responsibilities.

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** What is the role of the main service in service composition?

**Good Answer:**

> The main service coordinates the business workflow by calling specialized services. It owns the overall process, while each supporting service handles its own specific responsibility.

---

# Key Takeaways

- One main service coordinates the workflow.
    
- Supporting services perform specialized tasks.
    
- Composition creates modular and maintainable systems.
    
- Each service keeps a single responsibility.
