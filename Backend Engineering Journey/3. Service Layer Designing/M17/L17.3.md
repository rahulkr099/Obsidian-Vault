# Module 17 — Lesson 17.3: Designing Composed Workflows ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn how to decide which service should coordinate a business workflow.

A common mistake is letting **every service call every other service**.

Instead:

> **Choose one service to own the workflow.**

---

# Example 1 — Blog App

Requirement:

> User publishes a post.

✅ Good Design

```text
PostService

↓

SlugService

↓

PostRepository

↓

SearchService

↓

NotificationService
```

`PostService` owns the entire publishing workflow.

---

# Example 2 — E-commerce

Requirement:

> Customer places an order.

✅ Good Design

```text
OrderService

↓

InventoryService

↓

PaymentService

↓

NotificationService
```

`OrderService` is responsible for the complete order process.

---

# Wrong Design

❌ Every service controls part of the workflow.

```text
OrderService

↓

PaymentService

↓

InventoryService

↓

OrderService
```

Now it's difficult to understand:

- Who owns the workflow?
    
- Who starts the transaction?
    
- Who handles failures?
    

---

# Ask This Question

Whenever you design a feature:

> **"Which service owns this business action?"**

Examples:

|Business Action|Main Service|
|---|---|
|Publish post|`PostService`|
|Place order|`OrderService`|
|Register user|`AuthService`|
|Complete task|`TaskService`|

That service becomes the **orchestrator**.

---

# Best Practices

✅ One workflow → One main service.

✅ Supporting services perform specialized work.

✅ Keep ownership of the workflow clear.

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** How do you decide which service should coordinate a workflow?

**Good Answer:**

> I identify the main business action. The service responsible for that business domain becomes the orchestrator and coordinates other specialized services needed to complete the workflow.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Every workflow should have **one owner**.
    
- The owner coordinates other services.
    
- Avoid shared ownership of workflows.
    
- Clear ownership makes systems easier to understand and maintain.
