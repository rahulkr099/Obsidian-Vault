# Module 17 — Lesson 17.4: Service Composition Best Practices ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn the practical rules for composing services in a professional backend architecture.

This lesson summarizes Module 17.

---

# Rule 1 — One Workflow, One Orchestrator

Every business workflow should have **one main service**.

✅ Good

```text
OrderService

↓

InventoryService

↓

PaymentService

↓

NotificationService
```

`OrderService` owns the workflow.

---

# Rule 2 — One Service, One Responsibility

Each supporting service should have one job.

```text
PaymentService

↓

Process Payment
```

```text
NotificationService

↓

Send Notification
```

Don't mix responsibilities.

---

# Rule 3 — Don't Build God Services

❌ Bad

```text
AppService

↓

Users

Posts

Orders

Payments

Emails
```

✅ Better

```text
UserService

OrderService

PaymentService

EmailService
```

Small services are easier to understand and test.

---

# Rule 4 — Communicate Through Public Methods

A service should only call another service's **public methods**.

```text
PostService

↓

NotificationService.send(dto)
```

Don't depend on another service's internal implementation.

---

# Rule 5 — Keep the Workflow Easy to Read

A good workflow should read like a business process.

Example:

```text
Place Order

↓

Validate Order

↓

Reserve Stock

↓

Process Payment

↓

Create Order

↓

Send Confirmation
```

If another developer can read the workflow and understand the business process, your composition is clear.

---

# Common Mistakes

### ❌ Every Service Calls Every Other Service

```text
Service A

↔

Service B

↔

Service C
```

This creates tight coupling.

---

### ❌ Large Services

One service containing dozens of unrelated responsibilities.

---

### ❌ No Clear Workflow Owner

If it's unclear which service owns the business action, the architecture becomes difficult to maintain.

---

# Interview Questions

### Q1. What is service composition?

**Answer:**

> Service composition is the practice of building a business workflow by coordinating multiple small, focused services instead of putting all logic into one large service.

---

### Q2. Who should own a composed workflow?

**Answer:**

> The service responsible for the main business action should own and coordinate the workflow.

---

### Q3. Why is service composition better than one large service?

**Answer:**

> It improves maintainability, reusability, testing, and separation of responsibilities by keeping each service focused on a single purpose.

# Practice Exercise

Design the workflow for:

> **A user registers an account.**

A clean composition:

```text
AuthService
        │
        ▼
UserService
(Create user)
        │
        ▼
EmailService
(Send verification email)
        │
        ▼
NotificationService
(Create welcome notification)
```

`AuthService` owns the workflow.

The supporting services each perform one specialized responsibility.
