# Lesson 4.1 — What Are Business Rules?

> **Goal:** Learn to distinguish business rules from technical code.

---

## What is a Business Rule?

A business rule defines **how your application should behave**.

It answers questions like:

- Who can perform this action?
    
- When is the action allowed?
    
- What should happen after the action?
    

A business rule is **not** about Express, MongoDB, or HTTP.

---

# Example 1 — Blog App

Requirement:

> Users can publish blog posts.

Backend engineers immediately ask:

```text
Who can publish?

↓

Only the owner?

↓

Can an already published post be published again?

↓

Should publishedAt be updated?

↓

Should followers be notified?
```

These are business rules.

---

# Example 2 — URL Shortener

Requirement:

> Users can create short URLs.

Possible business rules:

```text
User must be logged in.

↓

Custom alias must be unique.

↓

Free users can create up to 100 URLs.

↓

Expiration date cannot be in the past.
```

---

# Example 3 — E-commerce

Requirement:

> Customer places an order.

Business rules:

```text
Product must exist.

↓

Stock must be available.

↓

Coupon must be valid.

↓

Payment must succeed.

↓

Order confirmation should be sent.
```

---

# Technical Logic vs Business Logic

|Technical Logic|Business Logic|
|---|---|
|Read `req.body`|User must own the post|
|Verify JWT|Premium users get custom aliases|
|Connect to MongoDB|Coupon must not be expired|
|Return `200 OK`|Order cannot be cancelled after shipping|

A simple way to remember it:

> **If the company changes its policy, business logic changes.**

For example:

Yesterday:

```text
Free users can create 100 URLs.
```

Today:

```text
Free users can create 50 URLs.
```

Only the **service** changes.

The controller and repository remain the same.

---

# How Backend Engineers Read Requirements

Beginners often read this:

> "Users can publish posts."

and immediately start coding.

Professional engineers first identify the rules.

Example:

```text
Requirement

↓

Business Rules

↓

Service Design

↓

Repository Calls

↓

Code
```

They think before they code.

---

# Where Do Business Rules Live?

Almost always in the **Service Layer**.

Example:

```text
Controller

↓

PostService.publishPost()

↓

Repository
```

The controller doesn't decide _if_ publishing is allowed.

The service does.

# Quick Practice

For each statement, decide if it is **Business Logic** or **Technical Logic**.

|Statement|Type|
|---|---|
|Read `req.params.id`|?|
|User cannot enroll twice|?|
|Verify JWT token|?|
|Premium users can upload larger files|?|
|Return `404 Not Found`|?|
|Coupon expires after 30 days|?|

### Answers

- Read `req.params.id` → **Technical Logic**
    
- User cannot enroll twice → **Business Logic**
    
- Verify JWT token → **Technical Logic**
    
- Premium users can upload larger files → **Business Logic**
    
- Return `404 Not Found` → **Technical Logic**
    
- Coupon expires after 30 days → **Business Logic**
    

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** What is business logic?

**Good Answer:**

> Business logic is the set of rules that define how the application behaves. It determines what actions are allowed, when they are allowed, and what should happen as part of a business workflow. In a layered backend architecture, business logic typically belongs in the Service Layer.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Business rules define **application behavior**, not technical implementation.
    
- Requirements should be analyzed before writing code.
    
- Most business rules belong in the **Service Layer**.
    
- A good backend engineer spends time understanding the rules before implementing them.
