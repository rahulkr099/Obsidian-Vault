# Module 4 — Lesson 4.2: Breaking Requirements into Business Rules ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn the professional process of converting a product requirement into backend logic before writing any code.

This is one of the most valuable backend engineering skills.

A junior developer hears a requirement and starts coding.

A professional backend engineer hears a requirement and starts asking questions.

---

# The 5-Step Framework

Whenever you receive a requirement, follow this process:

```text
Requirement
      ↓
Business Rules
      ↓
Edge Cases
      ↓
Service Method
      ↓
Repository Calls
```

Never jump directly to coding.

---

# Example 1 — Blog App

## Requirement

> Users can publish blog posts.

Don't write code yet.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Business Rules

Ask:

```text
Does the post exist?

↓

Is the user logged in?

↓

Does the user own the post?

↓

Is the post still a draft?

↓

Should publishedAt be updated?

↓

Should followers be notified?
```

Now the requirement is much clearer.

---

## Step 2 — Identify Edge Cases

Think:

```text
Post not found

Already published

Unauthorized user

Database failure
```

Professionals think about failure paths before success paths.

---

## Step 3 — Design the Service

```javascript
publishPost({
    userId,
    postId
})
```

Notice:

No Express.

No `req`.

Only business data.

---

## Step 4 — Repository Calls

The service may need:

```text
PostRepository.findById()

↓

PostRepository.update()
```

Maybe also:

```text
NotificationRepository.create()
```

# Mapping Rules to Layers

|Task|Layer|
|---|---|
|Read `req.body`|Controller|
|Check ownership|Service|
|Validate business rule|Service|
|Find post|Repository|
|Save changes|Repository|
|Return HTTP response|Controller|

This mapping should become automatic in your mind.

# The Rule Extraction Habit

Whenever you hear words like:

- only
    
- must
    
- cannot
    
- should
    
- before
    
- after
    
- maximum
    
- minimum
    
- allowed
    
- required
    

You are probably looking at a business rule.

Example:

> Only premium users can schedule posts.

Immediately think:

```text
Premium check

↓

Schedule validation

↓

Save schedule
```

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** A product manager says, "Users can cancel an order." What do you do first?

**Good Answer:**

> Before writing code, I identify the business rules and edge cases. For example: Can every order be cancelled? Is cancellation allowed after shipping? Should inventory be restored? Should a refund be processed? Once those rules are clear, I design the service method and repository interactions.

This answer demonstrates that you think like a backend engineer, not just a programmer.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Never jump directly from **requirement → code**.
    
- Break every requirement into:
    
    1. Business rules
        
    2. Edge cases
        
    3. Service method
        
    4. Repository interactions
        
- Ask questions before making assumptions.
    
- Strong backend design starts with understanding the problem, not writing code.
