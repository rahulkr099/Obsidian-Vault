# Module 5 — Lesson 5.2: Extracting Business Actions ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn how to identify the business actions hidden inside a user story.

A user story often looks simple, but it may contain **one or more business actions**.

---

# Step 1 — Read the User Story

Example:

> **As a user, I want to publish my blog post.**

Don't think about Express or MongoDB.

Ask:

> **"What action is the user trying to perform?"**

Answer:

```text
Publish Post
```

---

# Step 2 — Convert the Action into a Service Method

```text
Publish Post

↓

publishPost()
```

The service method should represent the business action.

---

# One User Story Can Have Multiple Actions

Example:

> Customer cancels an order.

Possible actions:

```text
cancelOrder()

↓

refundPayment()

↓

sendCancellationEmail()
```

The **main service method** is:

```text
cancelOrder()
```

It may internally coordinate other actions.

---

# Ask the Right Questions

For every user story, ask:

```text
What is the main business action?

↓

Who performs it?

↓

What is being acted upon?
```

Example:

> Teacher grades an assignment.

```text
Who?

↓

Teacher

What?

↓

Assignment

Action?

↓

Grade
```

Service:

```text
gradeAssignment()
```

---

# Common Beginner Mistake

Thinking in HTTP:

```text
POST /publish

PUT /posts

DELETE /users
```

Professional thinking:

```text
publishPost()

updatePost()

deleteUser()
```

The service is designed around the **business**, not the HTTP verb.

---

# Best Practices

✅ Use **verbs** that describe business actions.

✅ Keep method names clear and specific.

✅ Design methods before writing controllers.

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** How do you derive service methods from a user story?

**Good Answer:**

> I identify the main business action in the user story and design a service method around that action. The method name should describe the business capability, such as `publishPost()` or `placeOrder()`, rather than an HTTP operation.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Every user story contains one or more business actions.
    
- Convert business actions into service methods.
    
- Think in business language, not HTTP language.
    
- The service method should clearly describe what the system does.
