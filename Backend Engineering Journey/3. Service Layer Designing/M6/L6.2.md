# Module 6 — Lesson 6.2: Designing Business-Centric Methods ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

> **Goal:** Learn how to design service methods that represent business capabilities, not technical operations.

A service method should answer one question:

> **"What business action does this perform?"**

---

# Think in Business Language

Good method names describe what the business understands.

### ✅ Good

```text
createPost()

publishPost()

archivePost()

restorePost()

deletePost()
```

A product manager can almost understand these names.

---

### ❌ Bad

```text
process()

execute()

handle()

updateData()

doWork()
```

These names don't explain what the method actually does.

---

# Compare

### Beginner

```text
update()
```

Question:

> Update what?

---

### Professional

```text
updatePost()
```

Now everyone understands it.

# One Method = One Business Capability

Avoid methods that do many unrelated things.

❌ Bad

```text
managePost(action)
```

Example:

```text
managePost("publish")

managePost("archive")

managePost("delete")
```

Better:

```text
publishPost()

archivePost()

deletePost()
```

Each method has one clear purpose.

---

# Think Like a Product Manager

If a product manager says:

> Users can restore deleted posts.

Your first thought should be:

```text
restorePost()
```

Not:

```text
updatePost()
```

The method should match the business feature.

---

# Best Practices

✅ Use meaningful verbs (`create`, `publish`, `cancel`, `approve`).

✅ Include the business object (`Post`, `Order`, `Task`).

✅ Keep one method focused on one business action.

---

# Interview Question

**Q:** How do you choose names for service methods?

**Good Answer:**

> I name service methods after business capabilities, not technical operations. A method like `publishPost()` is much clearer than a generic method like `execute()` because it communicates exactly what business action it performs.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Service methods should represent **business actions**.
    
- Avoid vague names like `process()` or `handle()`.
    
- One method should perform **one business capability**.
    
- Clear method names make your code easier to understand and maintain.
