# What is RBAC?

RBAC stands for:

> **Role-Based Access Control**

Instead of giving permissions directly to every user:

```text
Rahul

↓

Can Create Posts

Can Delete Posts

Can Ban Users

...
```

We assign a **role**.

```text
Rahul

↓

Author
```

The role determines what Rahul can do.

---

# Why Use Roles?

Imagine your Blog App has:

- 10 Users
    
- 100 Users
    
- 100,000 Users
    

Managing permissions for every individual user becomes difficult.

Instead:

```text
Admin

Author

Reader

Guest
```

Every user belongs to one of these roles.

# Permission Matrix

|Action|Guest|Reader|Author|Admin|
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
|View Posts|✅|✅|✅|✅|
|Create Posts|❌|❌|✅|✅|
|Edit Own Posts|❌|❌|✅|✅|
|Delete Own Posts|❌|❌|✅|✅|
|Delete Any Post|❌|❌|❌|✅|
|Manage Users|❌|❌|❌|✅|

This matrix becomes your authorization policy.

# Common Beginner Mistakes

## ❌ Using Roles Instead of Authentication

Wrong:

```text
Role Check

↓

No JWT Verification
```

Always authenticate first.

---

## ❌ Hardcoding Roles Everywhere

Bad:

```text
if(role=="admin")

...

if(role=="admin")
```

Centralize role checks in reusable middleware.

---

## ❌ Giving Admin Access to Everything Automatically

Not every admin should always have every permission.

Large applications often separate roles from permissions.

We'll learn that in Lesson 8.3.

---

## ❌ Skipping Ownership

Bad:

```text
Role = Author

↓

Delete Any Post
```

Authors should usually delete only **their own** posts.

---

## ❌ Returning 401

Remember:

Authentication succeeded.

Authorization failed.

Correct response:

```http
403 Forbidden
```

---

# Best Practices

- Authenticate before checking roles.
    
- Store roles in the user model.
    
- Keep role checks in middleware.
    
- Combine roles with ownership when needed.
    
- Keep controllers focused on business logic.
    
- Return **403 Forbidden** for authorization failures.

# Interview Questions

### Q1. What is RBAC?

**Answer:**

RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) is an authorization model where permissions are assigned to roles, and users receive permissions through their assigned roles.

---

### Q2. Where should role checks happen?

**Answer:**

Role checks are commonly implemented in **authorization middleware**, while ownership and other business-specific authorization rules often belong in the service layer.

---

### Q3. Can an Author delete every post?

**Answer:**

Usually no. An Author can typically delete only their own posts. An Admin may be allowed to delete any post.

---

### Q4. Why combine RBAC with ownership?

**Answer:**

Roles define broad capabilities (for example, "Author"), while ownership ensures users can act only on resources they own unless their role grants broader authority.

# 💡 Backend Engineering Tip

Think of authorization in **layers**:

```text
Authentication

↓

Who are you?

────────────────

RBAC

↓

What role do you have?

────────────────

Ownership

↓

Is this your resource?

────────────────

Business Rules

↓

Can this action happen now?
```

For example:

- **Authentication** verifies Rahul.
    
- **RBAC** confirms Rahul is an **Author**.
    
- **Ownership** checks that the post belongs to Rahul.
    
- **Business Rules** ensure the post isn't already archived or locked.
    

Separating these concerns makes your backend easier to maintain, test, and extend.
