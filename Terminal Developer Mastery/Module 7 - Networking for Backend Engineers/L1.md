# 1. What is a Network?

A **network** is simply a group of devices connected together so they can communicate and share data.

Devices can include:

- Computers
    
- Phones
    
- Servers
    
- Printers
    
- Smart TVs
    
- IoT devices
    

Example:

```text
Laptop ───── Router ───── Phone
      │
      └──── Desktop
```

All these devices form a small network.

---

# 2. What is LAN?

**LAN** stands for **Local Area Network**.

It is a network within a small geographical area.

Examples:

- Home Wi-Fi
    
- College computer lab
    
- Office network
    
- Cyber café
    

Example:

```text
          Home LAN

      Wi-Fi Router
      /    |     \
 Laptop Phone TV
      |
 Desktop
```

Characteristics:

- High speed
    
- Low latency
    
- Usually owned by one person or organization
    
- Uses private IP addresses
    

---

# 3. What is WAN?

**WAN** stands for **Wide Area Network**.

A WAN connects multiple LANs over long distances.

Examples:

- Company offices in different cities
    
- Banks
    
- Cloud providers
    
- The Internet
    

Example:

```text
Delhi Office ---- Internet ---- Mumbai Office
```

LAN = Small area

WAN = Large area

---

# 4. What is the Internet?

The **Internet** is simply a **global network of networks**.

Think of it like this:

```text
Home LAN
      \
Office LAN ---- Internet ---- Google Network
      /
College LAN
```

Every network is connected together through routers and Internet Service Providers (ISPs).

The Internet is not a single computer. It is millions of connected networks.

---

# 5. What is an ISP?

**ISP** stands for **Internet Service Provider**.

An ISP gives you access to the Internet.

Examples include companies that provide:

- Fiber broadband
    
- Mobile data
    
- Cable Internet
    

Without an ISP, your router cannot reach the Internet.

Example path:

```text
Laptop
   ↓
Home Router
   ↓
ISP
   ↓
Internet
   ↓
Google
```

---

# 6. What is a Modem?

A **modem** connects your home to your ISP.

Its job is to convert signals between your ISP's connection and your home network.

Example:

```text
Fiber Cable
      ↓
    Modem
      ↓
    Router
      ↓
 Devices
```

Many home devices combine the modem and router into a single unit.

---

# 7. What is a Router?

A **router** connects different networks.

At home, it connects:

```text
Your Home Network
        ↓
     Internet
```

The router decides where each packet should go.

Think of it as a traffic manager.

Example:

```text
Phone wants Google

Phone
  ↓
Router
  ↓
Internet
  ↓
Google
```

---

# 8. What is a Switch?

A **switch** connects devices **within the same LAN**.

Unlike a router, it does not connect to the Internet directly.

Example:

```text
PC1
   \
PC2 --- Switch --- Router --- Internet
   /
PC3
```

Large offices use switches to connect hundreds of computers.

---

# Router vs Switch

|Router|Switch|
|---|---|
|Connects different networks|Connects devices in the same network|
|Can access the Internet|Cannot access the Internet by itself|
|Uses IP addresses|Uses MAC addresses|
|Usually one per home|Many can exist in a network|

---

# 9. Public vs Private Networks

## Private Network

Only accessible within your local network.

Examples:

```text
192.168.x.x
10.x.x.x
172.16.x.x – 172.31.x.x
```

These IP addresses are **not routable on the public Internet**.

---

## Public Network

Accessible from anywhere on the Internet.

Examples:

```text
Google servers

Cloud servers

Your VPS
```

Your ISP usually assigns your home router a public IP.

---

# 10. Public IP vs Private IP

Example:

```text
Laptop

Private IP:
192.168.1.20
```

Router:

```text
Public IP:
49.xxx.xxx.xxx
```

The router performs **Network Address Translation (NAT)**, allowing multiple private devices to share one public IP.

```text
Laptop (192.168.1.20)
Phone  (192.168.1.21)
TV     (192.168.1.22)

        ↓

Router (Public IP)

        ↓

Internet
```

# 11. What Happens When You Type `https://google.com`?

Let's walk through the journey step by step.

## Step 1: Browser

You type:

```text
https://google.com
```

The browser begins the request.

---

## Step 2: DNS Lookup

The browser asks:

> "What is the IP address of `google.com`?"

A DNS server responds with an IP address.

Example:

```text
142.250.x.x
```

---

## Step 3: Create Packets

Your computer breaks the request into small pieces called **packets**.

Each packet includes:

- Source IP
    
- Destination IP
    
- Data
    
- Other networking information
    

---

## Step 4: Router

The packets reach your home router.

The router decides where to send them next.

---

## Step 5: ISP

Your ISP forwards the packets through many routers across the Internet.

Example:

```text
Home

↓

ISP

↓

Regional Router

↓

National Router

↓

International Router

↓

Google Network
```

---

## Step 6: Google Server

Google receives the request.

It processes it and prepares a response.

---

## Step 7: Response

The response travels back through the Internet to your browser.

---

## Step 8: Browser Displays the Page

The browser receives:

- HTML
    
- CSS
    
- JavaScript
    
- Images
    
- Fonts
    

It renders the webpage you see.

# Important Networking Terms

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|Network|Connected devices communicating with each other|
|LAN|Local Area Network|
|WAN|Wide Area Network|
|ISP|Internet Service Provider|
|Modem|Connects your home to the ISP|
|Router|Connects different networks and forwards packets|
|Switch|Connects devices within the same LAN|
|Packet|Small unit of data sent over a network|
|Public IP|Globally reachable IP address|
|Private IP|Local-only IP address|
|NAT|Allows private IPs to share a public IP|

# Practical Lab

## View your private IP

```bash
ip addr
```

or

```bash
hostname -I
```

Example output:

```text
192.168.1.20
```

---

## View your public IP

```bash
curl ifconfig.me
```

Example:

```text
49.xxx.xxx.xxx
```

Compare the two addresses. Notice that your private IP and public IP are different because your router uses NAT.

---

## Check your network interfaces

```bash
ip link
```

You'll typically see interfaces such as:

- `lo` (loopback)
    
- `eth0` or `enp...` (Ethernet)
    
- `wlan0` or `wlp...` (Wi-Fi)
    

We'll study these in a later lesson.

# Real Backend Example

Imagine your Express.js API is running on port `5000`:

```text
http://192.168.1.20:5000
```

- Other devices on the same LAN may be able to access it.
    
- Someone on the Internet **cannot** access it unless you expose it with port forwarding, a reverse proxy, or deploy it to a public server.
    

This distinction between private and public networks is fundamental when deploying backend applications.

# Common Interview Questions

**1. What is the difference between LAN and WAN?**

A LAN connects devices in a small area (like a home or office), while a WAN connects multiple LANs across larger geographic areas.

**2. What is the Internet?**

The Internet is a worldwide network of interconnected networks that communicate using the TCP/IP protocol suite.

**3. What is the role of a router?**

A router connects different networks and forwards packets toward their destination using routing information.

**4. Why do we need an ISP?**

An ISP provides the connection between your local network and the global Internet.

**5. Why does my laptop have a different IP from my home's public IP?**

Your laptop uses a private IP inside your LAN. Your router translates it to a public IP using NAT before sending traffic to the Internet.

---

# Key Takeaways

- A **network** connects devices so they can communicate.
    
- A **LAN** is a local network; a **WAN** connects networks over long distances.
    
- The **Internet** is a global network of networks.
    
- An **ISP** gives your network Internet access.
    
- A **modem** connects your home to the ISP.
    
- A **router** forwards traffic between networks.
    
- A **switch** connects devices within the same LAN.
    
- Private IP addresses are used inside local networks, while public IP addresses are reachable on the Internet.
    
- Visiting a website involves DNS resolution, packet routing, server processing, and a response back to your browser.
